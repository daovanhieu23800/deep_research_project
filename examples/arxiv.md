## IV. Root Cause Analysis

Stagnant productivity in the logistics sector can be attributed to multiple primary and contributing factors. Our analysis highlights these key influences:

### Primary Drivers of Stagnant Productivity

**1. Technological Limitations**
- A significant barrier is the outdated technological infrastructure used across logistics operations. Technology problems, such as high costs for upgrades, cybersecurity risks, and complex integration with legacy systems, are prevalent [17]. These issues hinder the adoption of innovative solutions that can enhance efficiency and transparency.
- Furthermore, the industry’s slow investment in automation—growing at only 3-5% annually for warehouse solutions—illustrates a hesitance that stymies potential efficiency gains [19].

**2. Skills and Training Gaps**
- The logistics sector experiences a substantial skills gap, driven by a lack of formal education programs and rapid technological evolution, requiring continual upskilling [22]. This gap leads to inefficiencies and increased costs, which directly affect productivity [20].
- Training deficiencies have been documented extensively, with reports highlighting inadequate programs that fail to evolve with industry demands and technological advancements [21].

### Contributions from Station Location and Coverage

**Station Coverage and Productivity**
- Although specific data from the internal query was unavailable, broader industry insights suggest that optimal station coverage is crucial for operational efficiency. Stations with expansive coverage capabilities can strategically place inventory closer to demand areas, thereby reducing transportation time and costs [15].
- Locations that are not optimally distributed may struggle with these cost-efficiencies and thus contribute to stagnant company-wide productivity.

### Technological and Training Influence on Productivity

- **Technological Shortcomings:** As detailed, the lack of modern technology directly influences productivity by failing to streamline operations and complicate the supply chain [18]. When systems are not intelligently integrated, it leads to increased time and effort spent on operations that could be automated or enhanced through technology.

- **Training Shortcomings:** Training ineffectiveness also plays a crucial role in stagnant productivity. Approximately 60% of logistics roles reported enhanced performance post-training, illustrating that effective programs can significantly boost productivity. However, 50% of employees reported inadequate time to apply new skills learned, indicating a mismatch in training provision and real-world application opportunities [20].

### Conclusion
The primary drivers of stagnant productivity center around technological and training shortcomings, with station location playing a significant competitive role in logistics efficiency. Targeted investment in modern technologies and comprehensive training programs could address these core issues, fostering considerable productivity improvements.
